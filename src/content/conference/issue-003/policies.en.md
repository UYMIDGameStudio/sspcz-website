This page summarises the current submission, review, academic-integrity and AI-use rules for the 3rd SSPCZ (SSPCZ 2026). The resources library provides the [Paper Review Guidelines](/documents/paper-review-guidelines.docx) and the required [Academic Integrity and Ethics Declaration](/documents/academic-ethics-declaration.docx), currently available in Chinese. If the Academic Team issues a later formal notice, the latest notice prevails.

## Deadline and Required Materials

- The provisional deadline is 20 September 2026 at 23:59, subject to formal notice.
- Papers submitted on or before 15 September may enter minor or major revision and re-review.
- Papers submitted from 16 to 20 September follow the normal review process but have no revision or re-review stage.
- Every submission must include a fully signed Academic Integrity and Ethics Declaration. A paper will normally not be accepted if the declaration is missing, inaccurate or incomplete.

## Review Principles

Papers must be original, unpublished work. They must not be under simultaneous submission to another conference, journal or academic body, or already in another review process. Review is anonymous. Each paper is normally assessed independently by two or three reviewers, and the result takes effect after confirmation by the Academic Team lead or deputy lead.

## Review Criteria (50 points)

| Criterion | Points | Main consideration |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Argumentation | 12 | Claims, evidence, logic and persuasiveness |
| Theoretical depth and reflection on conclusions | 12 | Topic, problem awareness, theoretical depth and reflection |
| Originality and independent thinking | 10 | Novelty of the view and independent thought |
| Conceptual understanding and philosophical literacy | 6 | Accuracy of concepts and philosophical understanding |
| Academic standards | 6 | Accurate citation: 4; formatting compliance: 2 |
| Language and expression | 4 | Diction, register and clarity |

Fabricated citations are academic misconduct and trigger verification. A paper with no citations is not automatically misconduct, but its academic-standards score may be affected.

## Review Outcomes

| Outcome | Score | Treatment |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Accept | ≥38 | Considered for a presentation slot |
| Minor revision | 28–37 | Revised and re-reviewed |
| Major revision | 18–27 | Substantially revised and re-reviewed |
| Reject | <18 | Rejected |

For two reviews, the mean score is considered together with the consistency of the decisions. For three reviews, the mean or median may be used alongside the majority view. A difference of eight points or more, or a clear conflict in direction, is referred to the Academic Team lead or deputy lead.

Papers submitted from 16 to 20 September have no revision stage: accepted papers enter presentation selection, minor-revision papers become attending papers, and major-revision or rejected papers are rejected.

## AI Use and Disclosure

AI may assist with language polishing, translation, reference management and similar tasks, but it may not replace an author's core thinking or argument. AI cannot be listed as an author or co-author, or as the author of a citation, note or reference.

Authors who use AI must disclose that use consistently in all three places: the cover letter, acknowledgements and methods section. The disclosure must identify the tool and version, its exact use and the complete prompts. An omission in any one place will be returned for correction. Authors who did not use AI need only make the corresponding declaration in the Academic Integrity and Ethics Declaration.

AI-generated main-text content is normally handled as follows:

- Up to 15%: normal review;
- 15%–30%: revise and resubmit for re-review; rejection follows if the revised paper still exceeds the threshold;
- Above 30%: normally rejected outright.

Where a detection result is clearly disputed, the Academic Team will consider human review and the author's explanation. AI use is a compliance question and does not form part of the 50-point academic score.

## Rejection, Withdrawal and Appeal

A paper may be rejected for a reject decision, unsuccessful re-review, AI-generated content beyond the permitted range, failure to correct formatting on time, a risk-review trigger or another failure to meet submission requirements.

Verified plagiarism, self-plagiarism, duplicate submission, fabricated citations or data, or concealment of substantial AI use may lead to a warning, rejection or withdrawal depending on severity. Acceptance or a presentation place may also be cancelled. Authors may appeal a rejection or withdrawal in the manner and within the period announced by the Academic Team.

## Risk Review and Confidentiality

Papers must not incite hatred, violence or unlawful activity; target a group with discriminatory or insulting material; deliberately fabricate or spread false information; or violate applicable law and policy. A paper that triggers risk review will normally be rejected and returned.

The Academic Team and reviewers must keep papers, author identities, reviews and the review process confidential. They may not disclose them to a third party without authorisation.
